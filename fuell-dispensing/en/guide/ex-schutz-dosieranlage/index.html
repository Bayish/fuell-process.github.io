Ex protection for dispensing systems: what matters

Anyone who dispenses solvent-based paints, coatings, printing inks or adhesives almost always works in potentially explosive areas. Explosion protection is then not an option but a prerequisite – for the dispensing system as well as for storage, agitators and terminals.

Why explosion protection?

Many solvents form vapours even at room temperature which, mixed with air, create an ignitable mixture. One ignition source – a spark, a hot surface, an electrostatic discharge – is enough. At Sherwin-Williams in El Salvador, explosion protection was a central requirement of the management, because some of the solvents used become critical at temperatures that are reached there every day.

ATEX in brief

In the EU, two directives regulate explosion protection (ATEX):

  • The product directive 2014/34/EU applies to manufacturers of equipment and protective systems used in potentially explosive areas.
  • The operator directive 1999/92/EC obliges employers to divide areas into zones according to the hazard and to use suitable equipment.

For gases and vapours there are zones 0, 1 and 2 – from a permanent to a rare and short-term hazard. The operator defines which zone applies in the explosion protection document.

What this means for the dispensing system

  • All equipment in the zone must be suitable for it: valve drives, scales, agitators, terminals.
  • Closed systems reduce vapours: raw materials come from tanks through pipes instead of from open containers.
  • Earthing and equipotential bonding prevent electrostatic charging.
  • Operator terminals are explosion-protected or placed outside the zone.

Ex protection at Füll

Füll holds an EC certificate according to Annex IV of Directive 2014/34/EU for the manufacture of various items of equipment, monitored by TÜV SÜD Product Service. Füll already received the manufacturing approval for agitators and level indicators according to ATEX in 2003. The compact dispensing centre FS XS is suitable for Ex and non-Ex environments.

In the Sherwin-Williams project, all systems and equipment were built according to ATEX, and nine further dispensing systems were connected to a central control system with explosion-protected terminals. The result from the customer’s point of view: closed systems, more cleanliness and operational safety.

Checklist for operators

  • Explosion protection document and zone classification up to date?
  • All components of the system suitable and marked for the zone?
  • Closed storage and conveying wherever possible?
  • Earthing concept for containers, scales and pipes?
  • Maintenance by qualified personnel with Ex knowledge?

Frequently asked questions

Who defines the Ex zone?

The operator, as part of the risk assessment and the explosion protection document. The system builder then designs the system for this zone.

Are there Füll systems for Ex and non-Ex areas?

Yes. The FS XS, for example, is suitable for both environments; larger systems are designed for the specific project.
