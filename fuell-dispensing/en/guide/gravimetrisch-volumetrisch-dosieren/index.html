Gravimetric or volumetric dispensing: which method when?

Terms

  • Gravimetric dispensing

Whether a dispensing system measures mass or volume determines accuracy, speed and traceability. Both methods have their place – and in many systems they are combined.

The principle: mass or volume

In gravimetric dispensing, the container stands on a scale. The valve opens until the target mass is reached. What is measured is therefore exactly what is written in the recipe – because recipes in the paint, coatings and chemical industries are usually given in mass fractions.

In volumetric dispensing, a defined volume is delivered, for example by a piston pump. The mass is calculated from the density.

Gravimetric: the strengths

  • Independent of density and temperature: the scale measures the actual mass.
  • High reproducibility: every addition is checked and logged.
  • Flexible with many components: a dispensing centre can dispense many raw materials one after the other into the same container.
  • Coarse and fine flow: first dispensing with a high flow rate, then only fine shortly before the target (coarse and fine flow dispensing).

The disadvantage: components are usually dispensed one after the other. With many components per recipe, the process therefore takes longer than simultaneous dispensing.

Volumetric: the strengths

  • Simultaneous dispensing: several components at the same time, for example pigmented tinting pastes into a pre-filled container.
  • Very fast with small quantities.
  • No weighing set-up required.

The disadvantage: deviations in density, air bubbles or temperature fluctuations have a direct effect on the mass. Many liquids expand measurably when heated – the same volume then contains less mass.

Direct comparison

CriterionGravimetricVolumetric
Measured variableMass (scale, mass flow meter)Volume (piston pump, dispensing unit)
Influence of density and temperaturelowhigh
Sequencemostly one after the otheralso simultaneous
Typical applicationProduction from many raw materials and semi-finished productsTinting into pre-filled containers, small quantities
Traceabilityevery mass measuredcalculated, optionally checked gravimetrically

Influencing factors in practice

Accuracy does not only depend on the measuring principle. Just as important are:

  • The dispensing valve: outlet size and design must match flow behaviour, density and viscosity. Füll adapts the valves individually to the substances to be dispensed.
  • The valve drive: servo-driven valves open precisely and reproducibly.
  • The homogeneity of the raw material: if pigment settles, the composition is wrong – no matter how accurately it is dispensed. That is why components are recirculated (recirculation).
  • Run-on and drops at the valve tip, especially with viscous media.

Combined solutions

In many systems both principles work together. With Füll in-can tinting, the base is first added gravimetrically and then tinted volumetrically and simultaneously with pastes. The U-VX5 dispenses UV-curing inks simultaneously or under gravimetric control. The Füll dispensing centres FS 2005.2 and FS 182.2 can be equipped with scales or with the volumetric dispensing unit HX5.

How to make the decision

  • Many raw materials, production from semi-finished products, high traceability requirements: gravimetric.
  • Tinting into pre-filled containers, high speed, few pastes: volumetric.
  • Both in the same plant: a combined system or two types of system with shared software.

Frequently asked questions

Which method is more accurate?

In terms of mass, gravimetric dispensing is usually more accurate because density and temperature play no role. Volumetric systems also achieve good values under constant conditions.

Can you dispense volumetrically and still weigh?

Yes. Volumetric dispensing can additionally be checked with a scale, so traceability is maintained.
